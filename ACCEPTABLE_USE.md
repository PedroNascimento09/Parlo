# Acceptable use

Parlo changes how you sound to other people. That is powerful, so the limits are explicit.

## Allowed

- Translating your own speech in meetings, classes, calls, streams, and recordings.
- Translating a conversation you are part of, with the other participants aware that translation is being used.
- Dubbing your own videos into another language.
- Accessibility use, such as following a talk in a language you do not speak.

## Not allowed

- Impersonating another person, or presenting a translated voice as theirs.
- Fraud, scams, or deceiving someone about who they are speaking to.
- Recording or translating a conversation you are not part of, or one where you have no consent to do so.
- Circumventing a platform's terms of service or a lawful interception restriction.
- Harassment, threats, or content that is illegal in your jurisdiction.

## Disclosure

Tell the people you speak with that your voice is being translated. It is the decent thing to do, and in many places a legal requirement. The app can play a short spoken notice at the start of a call if you enable it.

## Voice cloning limits

Parlo only translates the voice of the person speaking live. It does not accept a recording as a source for a synthetic voice, and it does not offer a way to clone someone else. Feature requests that would break this rule are declined.

## Enforcement

The maintainers may close issues, reject contributions, or block accounts that promote misuse. Nothing in the MIT license grants permission to use the software unlawfully; the license governs the code, this policy governs the community.

## Reporting misuse

Open an issue without personal data, or contact the maintainers privately for anything sensitive.
