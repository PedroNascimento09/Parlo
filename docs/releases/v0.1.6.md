# v0.1.6

Parlo is a translator that lives entirely on your machine. You speak Portuguese and the person in the call hears English, or the other way round, with no account, no cloud and no audio ever leaving the computer. This version is the first one anyone can install and actually use: the download is self contained, the first screen offers the one thing that has to be downloaded once, and everything after that works with the network cable pulled out.

It is also the version where Parlo stops being a single trick. You can now dub a video file in Portuguese and get it back in English with subtitles, watch translated captions as someone talks, leave it listening during a call, route the translated voice to any speaker the machine has, and choose the language direction and the speech recogniser from inside the app instead of a command line. Underneath, the local service grew a configuration file, a token, and a defence against the one attack that matters for a program listening on your own port: a web page in your browser trying to talk to it.

## What it feels like to use

You open Parlo and, the first time, it says it needs about 800 MB of models and offers a button. It downloads them, tells you how long it has been at it, and when it finishes it says the app works offline from now on. From then on the screen is one card: hold the space bar or click, speak, and the translated sentence appears in front of you while the translated voice plays back. A line under the card tells you how long the last translation took, end to end, so you never have to guess.

If you would rather watch than hear, captions mode writes the translation as text and never speaks. If you leave the Live switch on, Parlo keeps listening, translating each couple of seconds of speech and speaking it in turn, which is what you want when someone else is doing the talking. If you have a file, you drop a video on the page and it comes back dubbed, with an `.srt` next to it; music and effects stay underneath, ducked while the voice speaks, so the original does not disappear entirely.

For a call, the honest answer today is still a virtual cable: install one, run `parlo speakers` to find it, and `parlo listen --play --speaker N` sends the translated voice into the meeting app as if it were a microphone. A real Parlo microphone, one that appears in Zoom and Meet by itself, is the next milestone and is not in this version.

## What was measured

Every number below comes from the reference machine, an ordinary 12 core desktop with no GPU, running Windows 11, four threads, and the measurement commands that ship in the repository. Nothing here is extrapolated.

Recognition runs at a real time factor of **0.113** with Parakeet against **0.309** for Whisper base on the same clip, which is why Parakeet is the default. On 24 Portuguese sentences read back by a synthetic voice, the word error rate is **0.215** clean, **0.199** at 20 dB of noise and **0.197** at 10 dB; Whisper small managed **0.217**, **0.242** and **0.309**, so the two are equal on clean audio and Parakeet holds up better as it gets noisy. A thirty minute run of looped speech processed **1095 segments** at **420.7 ms** each with no errors and no drift, and the last ten cycles were 58 ms faster than the first ten. Cold start, with every model loaded from disk, is **18.1 s**.

Captions cost **958 to 1266 ms** of processing on a 1.2 second window, so a caption appears about **2.2 s** after the words start. Dubbing a 6.6 second Portuguese clip produced English audio peaking at **-0.5 dBFS**. With the background kept, the original sits at 0.55 between lines and drops to about 0.10 under the translated voice, with 0.15 second fades.

## Installing it

Download the archive for your system and unpack it. It contains `parlo`, a folder called `parlo-engine`, and this README; keep the folder next to the program, because that is where the translator lives. Run `parlo app`, let it download the models once, and you are done.

If you would rather not run it from your downloads folder, `parlo install` copies everything into a per user directory and adds a Start Menu entry on Windows or an application menu entry on Linux, with its own icon. `parlo uninstall` takes it away again and leaves your models in place unless you add `--purge`.

On Linux and macOS the same archive is built by the same workflow, but only Windows has been exercised by hand. The other two are covered by unit tests and CI builds and have never been run on a real desktop, which is stated plainly rather than discovered by you.

## What does not work yet

Dubbing timing is approximate: a translation that does not fit its slot is spoken up to 18 percent faster and can still overlap the next line. `--keep-background` keeps music and effects but performs no source separation, so the original voice is faintly there between sentences. There is no virtual microphone, no tray icon and no global shortcut. Captions at 2.2 seconds miss the sub two second target the design set for slow machines, and reaching it costs accuracy. Only Portuguese and English have models, in both directions. The packaged engine is about 806 MB unpacked, mostly because the translation library imports torch through its sentence splitter.
